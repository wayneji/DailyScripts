# The Little Front Row

## Part 1: A Show at Work

Mike is getting ready for a family show at Monsters, Inc. Sulley helps him carry chairs into a room beside the laugh floor. Today the workers can bring their children to watch Mike tell jokes.

Mike puts a yellow chair near the door. It is for a little monster who needs to sit down while waiting.

"We have plenty of seats," Mike says. "Everyone will enjoy this."

Sulley looks at the chairs. The large ones are at the front because they are difficult to move.

"What about the small children?" he asks.

"They can hear me from the back," Mike says.

But Sulley is not sure that hearing is enough.

## Part 2: A Small Test

Before the families arrive, Sulley asks Mike to try the show. He puts a small toy on a back seat and sits in front of it.

Mike holds three red balls above his head. Then he drops one into his pocket and smiles.

"That is the funny part!" he says.

Sulley looks back at the toy. From its seat, Mike's hands are hidden behind Sulley's shoulders.

Mike walks to the back and bends down. Now he understands. A small child can hear the joke but cannot see where the ball goes.

"Perhaps the seats need a different plan," he says.

Together, they move the large chairs to the sides. They leave a clear path through the middle.

## Part 3: A Better View

Sulley puts a green mat at the front for the smallest children. Taller visitors sit on chairs behind them or at the sides. The yellow waiting chair stays by the door.

When the families arrive, Mike asks a little monster to check the view.

"I can see both your hands!" she says.

This time, everyone sees the ball fall into Mike's pocket. The children laugh before he even finishes his joke.

After the show, a father thanks Sulley for the new seats.

"Thank Mike too," Sulley says. "He tried looking from a smaller seat."

Mike smiles. His jokes are the same, but now more people can enjoy them.

## Useful A2 Words

- **view**: what you can see from a place.
- **shoulder**: the part of your body between your neck and your arm.
- **clear**: with nothing in the way.

## Reading Questions

1. Why is Sulley unsure about Mike's seating plan in Part 1?
   - A. Some workers may arrive without their children.
   - B. Small children may not see past the large chairs.
   - C. There may not be enough seats for the workers.

2. What does Mike learn from the test in Part 2?
   - A. The audience needs to see his hands to enjoy the joke.
   - B. The audience needs more time to find the room.
   - C. The audience needs Sulley to explain the joke.

3. Why does Mike ask the little monster about her view?
   - A. He wants her to choose a different joke.
   - B. He wants her to sit by the door.
   - C. He wants to check that the new plan works.

4. What helps Mike make the show better?
   - A. Telling a new joke about a lost ball.
   - B. Looking at a problem from someone else's place.
   - C. Asking the tallest visitors to stay at home.
