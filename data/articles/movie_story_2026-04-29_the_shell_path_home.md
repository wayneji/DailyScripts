# The Shell Path Home

Moana was sailing with her little sister, Simea, on a bright morning. The sea was blue and calm, and the wind was soft. They wanted to visit a small island with tall trees and white birds. Maui was not with them that day, so Moana was teaching Simea how to read the ocean.

"Look at the waves," Moana said. "They can show us where to go."

Simea watched carefully and smiled. She wanted to be brave like her sister.

When they reached the island, they walked along the beach and found many pretty shells. Simea ran ahead because she saw a red one near some rocks.

"Stay where I can see you," Moana called.

But then the wind changed, and dark clouds came across the sky. The sea was not calm now. Moana looked around and saw that Simea was gone.

"Simea!" she called.

There was no answer, only the sound of waves and birds.

Moana felt afraid, but she took a deep breath. "I must think," she said.

Then she saw a line of small shells on the sand. One, then another, then another. Simea often made little shell paths when she played.

Moana followed the shells around the rocks and up a small hill. At the top, she found Simea under a tree. The girl was holding her foot and trying not to cry.

"I slipped," Simea said softly. "I wanted to come back, but my foot hurts."

Moana sat beside her. "You did something very smart," she said. "The shells helped me find you."

The rain started to fall, so Moana made a quick cover with a big leaf and a piece of sailcloth from her bag. Then she helped Simea stand up. Slowly, they walked back together.

The boat moved hard on the water, but Moana stayed calm. She used the wind and waves carefully, and soon they were sailing home.

When they reached their island, Simea smiled. "I was scared," she said, "but I remembered what you teach me."

Moana hugged her. "Being brave does not mean never feeling fear," she said. "It means thinking clearly and finding the way home."

That day, both sisters learned that love, calm hearts, and small signs can guide people through trouble.
