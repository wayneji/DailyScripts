# Kids Audio Conversation: Moana 2 - The Tide Clock Race

Host 1: Hello, explorers! Welcome back to our morning story mission. Today we are sailing to a bright island for a new Moana adventure called "The Tide Clock Race." Are you ready for sun, sea, and clues?

Host 2: I am very ready. I can almost hear the waves already. This story is full of energy, teamwork, and a tiny mystery. Also, there are dancing crabs. Yes, dancing crabs. That is not a joke.

Host 1: Let’s jump in. The story starts very early in the morning. Moana wakes up before sunrise. The sea looks pink and gold, and a soft wind moves her hair. It feels calm and magical.

Host 2: Then we learn it is a huge day in the village. Everyone is preparing for Big Canoe Day. At noon, the village wants to sail together. Families are excited, children are smiling, and the beach is full of busy people.

Host 1: But then, boom, trouble arrives. Little Kiri runs to Moana and shouts that the tide clock is gone. The tide clock is a wooden wheel near the beach, and it tells people when the sea is safe.

Host 2: So this is serious. Without the tide clock, the children cannot join the canoe trip. The village needs that clock, and they need it quickly. Time is moving, and noon is getting closer every minute.

Host 1: I like Moana’s first reaction. She does not panic. She takes a deep breath and says, "It’s okay. We can find it." That is strong leadership. Calm first, action second. Very smart.

Host 2: And then she calls Maui. He appears in a bright flash from the trees. Classic Maui entrance. He smiles and says, "Did somebody ask for a very handsome hero?" Honestly, that line made me laugh.

Host 1: Moana gives him the perfect answer: "We need help, not jokes." But she laughs too, so the moment stays warm. That balance is lovely. She is focused, but she is still kind and friendly.

Host 2: Next comes clue hunting. They search the beach and look carefully at the sand. Moana notices tiny crab marks. Not bird marks, not shoe prints, but little crab tracks heading toward the black rocks.

Host 1: This part feels like a detective scene. Moana observes details. She does not guess wildly. She studies the ground, thinks clearly, and follows evidence. That is exactly how good problem-solvers work in real life too.

Host 2: They reach the black rocks and hear clicking sounds. Then they see something amazing. A group of small crabs is standing around the missing tide clock and using it as a dance floor. No way. Crab party!

Host 1: Can we pause for one second? The picture is brilliant. Tiny crabs tapping around a wooden wheel near dark rocks, with sea spray in the air. It is funny, vivid, and easy to imagine.

Host 2: Maui has a quick idea. He whispers that he can scare the crabs away with one big roar. Fast solution, maybe. But Moana says no. She chooses a kinder path, and that choice changes everything.

Host 1: Yes. She kneels down, speaks softly, and says, "Hello, little dancers. We need this clock today. Can we trade?" Her voice is polite and calm. She treats the crabs with respect, not fear.

Host 2: Then little Kiri helps too. He offers shiny shells from his pocket. That detail is so sweet. He is small, but he still makes a big difference. Everyone can help, even if they are young.

Host 1: The crabs look at the shells, stop dancing, and happily move away with their new treasures. Problem solved without shouting, fighting, or tears. Just observation, kindness, and a clever trade. What a win.

Host 2: Maui celebrates and shouts, "Teamwork wins!" I love that line. It is simple and true. Moana led, Kiri contributed, Maui supported, and even the crabs cooperated. Different characters, one shared success.

Host 1: Then comes the race against time. They roll the tide clock back to the beach. The sun is climbing higher, so they must move quickly. You can feel the urgency, but also the hope.

Host 2: And they make it. Just in time! The village claps and sings. Children jump into canoes, laughing. You can almost hear drums and splashing water. The whole place feels alive and full of joy.

Host 1: Moana then says a powerful line: "A strong leader listens, stays calm, and helps everyone." That sentence is the heart of this story. It is not only for heroes. It is for school, home, and friendships.

Host 2: Let’s unpack that for our listeners. First, "listens" means you pay attention to people and clues. Second, "stays calm" means you breathe and think before acting. Third, "helps everyone" means no one is left out.

Host 1: Exactly. Also, the story shows that speed is not always the best answer. Maui wanted a quick roar, but Moana chose communication. Sometimes a gentle plan works better than a loud plan.

Host 2: There is another lovely lesson about respect for nature. Moana does not treat the crabs as enemies. She sees them as living creatures with their own needs. She finds a way that works for both sides. That is a very grown-up choice, and it keeps peace on the beach.

Host 1: And I really like Kiri’s role. He starts the alarm, then gives shells for the trade. He is not just "the little kid" in the background. He is active, brave, and useful from beginning to end.

Host 2: If you are listening on your way to school, think about this: today you might be someone’s Kiri. Maybe you notice a small problem, share an idea, or help with a tiny action that saves the day.

Host 1: Ooh, challenge time. If your class loses something important, what would you do first? Would you shout, or would you look for clues, breathe, and ask others to help? Team detective mode sounds better, right? You can even make a tiny plan with three steps and share jobs.

Host 2: Very much better. And if you meet a "crab moment" - a situation where someone else has what you need - maybe try a fair trade or kind words before getting upset. It often works surprisingly well.

Host 1: Final picture from the story: bright sky, drums starting, canoes pushing into the sea, and friends sailing together. After a stressful morning, they move forward as one team. That ending feels wonderful.

Host 2: Memorable takeaway: Stay calm, be kind, and solve problems together.
