# Moana and the Small Star

Moana stood on the beach and looked at the sea. The water was calm, but the sky had many clouds. A little boy from the village held a wooden bowl and asked for help. He wanted to find a bright shell that his grandmother had dropped near the reef. It was a small thing, but it meant a lot to her.

Moana smiled and said, “We can find it together.” She took her canoe, and Pua came too. Soon they were moving over the soft waves. The wind was light, and the sun came out for a short time. The sea looked like silver.

When they reached the reef, they saw the problem. The shell was not on the sand. It was stuck inside a small rock pool where the water was too low to reach. Pua tried to jump in, but the pool was narrow and slippery.

Moana did not rush. She used a long palm leaf to pull the shell closer. Then the boy gave her a smooth stone, and she placed it under one side of the leaf so it could bend lower. After a few tries, the shell slid into her hand.

The boy clapped. “We found it!” he said.

On the way home, the clouds opened, and a warm rain started to fall. Moana and the boy laughed as the water dropped on their faces. The grandmother was waiting at the village shore. When she saw the shell, her eyes became wet with tears. She held it close and thanked them.

That night, the village had a small meal with fish, taro, and sweet fruit. The people sang near the fire, and the ocean moved gently in the dark.

Moana learned that a simple act can carry deep love. She also learned that the sea is not only for great journeys. Sometimes it is also for small kindnesses, one careful wave at a time.
