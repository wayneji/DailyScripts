# Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets: The Lost Key

It was a cold morning at Hogwarts, and Harry Potter was walking to breakfast with Ron Weasley. The castle looked dark after the rain, but the halls were warm and full of life. Harry liked the school very much, even on quiet days.

On the way, they met Professor Sprout near the greenhouse. She looked worried. “I need the silver key for the seed room,” she said. “I left it on my desk, but now it is gone. Without it, I cannot open the room for today’s lesson.”

Ron looked at Harry. Harry looked at the long hallway. “We can help,” Harry said.

They searched in the herb room first. They looked under tables, behind pots, and near the windows. But the key was not there. Then Hermione came in with a stack of books.

“You are looking for this?” she asked.

In her hand was a small silver key, but a little bird had tied a ribbon around it and carried it to the window. The bird was sitting on the shelf and pecking at crumbs.

Ron laughed. “That bird has very good taste in trouble.”

Harry walked slowly to the shelf. He did not scare the bird. He took one bread crumb from his pocket and placed it on the floor. The bird hopped down, and Harry gently took the key.

Professor Sprout smiled when they returned it. “Thank you. Now we can start the lesson.”

Inside the seed room, the students learned about small yellow plants that needed care and patience. Harry watered one plant, and Ron checked the labels. The lesson was simple, but it felt important.

After class, Harry thought about the morning. The key was small, but it still mattered. One small problem had almost stopped the whole lesson, and one calm idea had solved it.

He learned that kindness and patience are often better than hurry and noise. At Hogwarts, even a lost key can become a good day when friends help each other.
