# Elsa and the Warm Gloves

It was a cold morning in Arendelle. Snow covered the roofs, and the wind was soft but sharp. Inside the castle, Elsa sat near a window and watched the white street below. Anna was in the hall with a basket of warm bread and two pairs of wool gloves.

Today, Elsa wanted to walk to the market with Anna. She wanted to say hello to the people and help with small winter tasks. But Elsa had one worry. Her ice powers often came out when she felt nervous. If she touched the wrong thing, it might freeze.

Anna gave Elsa the gloves. “These will help,” she said.

Elsa smiled, but her hands still felt cold and tense. They went outside and walked slowly through the snow. Children waved. A man pulled a cart of fish. A girl asked for help to lift a sack of apples.

Elsa wanted to help first. She reached for the sack, but the cold air made her fingers shake. A little frost formed on the apples.

“Oh no,” Elsa said. “I am sorry.”

Anna shook her head. “It is fine. Let’s do it together.”

So Anna held the sack while Elsa used only her steady hands. The frost stopped. Then Elsa helped the girl pack the apples into a basket. She did not rush. She breathed slowly and kept her gloves on.

After that, Elsa and Anna visited the baker. Elsa used a small burst of ice to make the window clean and shiny, but only after Anna asked her to be careful. The baker laughed and thanked her.

By the end of the day, Elsa felt proud. She had not tried to be perfect. She had listened, stayed calm, and asked for help when she needed it.

That night, Elsa said, “I can do more when I do not rush.”

Anna smiled. “Yes. And I am always here.”

Elsa learned a simple lesson: calm hearts make better help, and good friends make hard days easier.
