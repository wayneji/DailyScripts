# Moana 2: The Tide Clock Race

Moana woke up before sunrise and looked at the sea. The water was pink and gold. A soft wind moved her hair. Today was special. The village was getting ready for the Big Canoe Day. Everyone wanted to sail together at noon.

Then little Kiri ran to Moana. "Moana! The tide clock is gone!" he cried.

The tide clock was a wooden wheel near the beach. It helped the village know when the sea was safe. Without it, the children could not join the canoe trip.

Moana took a deep breath. "It’s okay. We can find it. Maui!" she called.

A bright flash came from the trees. Maui jumped out, smiling. "Did somebody ask for a very handsome hero?"

"We need help, not jokes," Moana said, but she laughed.

They looked for clues. In the sand, Moana saw tiny crab marks. "They go to the black rocks," she said.

At the rocks, they heard clicking sounds. A group of small crabs stood around the tide clock. They were using it like a round dance floor.

Maui whispered, "I can scare them away. Big roar, done."

Moana shook her head. "No. Let’s be kind."

She knelt down and spoke softly. "Hello, little dancers. We need this clock today. Can we trade?"

Kiri offered shiny shells from his pocket. The crabs stopped, looked, and then moved away happily, carrying shells instead.

"Teamwork wins!" Maui cheered.

They rolled the tide clock back to the beach just as the sun climbed high. The village clapped and sang. Children jumped into canoes, laughing.

Moana smiled at the sea. "A strong leader listens, stays calm, and helps everyone," she said.

The drums began. The boats pushed off. Under the bright sky, Moana, Maui, and all their friends sailed forward together.
