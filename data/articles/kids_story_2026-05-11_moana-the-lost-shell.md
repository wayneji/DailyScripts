# Moana and the Lost Shell

Moana woke up early because the sea was shining like glass. Today was the island fair, and many families were preparing food, flowers, and music. Moana wanted to help her grandmother by bringing a special shell to the beach table.

The shell was small and white, and it had a pink line across one side. Moana loved it because her grandmother said it came from the first boat her family ever built.

"Please keep it safe," her grandmother said.

"I will," Moana said with a smile.

Moana put the shell in her pocket and walked to the beach. The air was warm, and the palm trees moved softly in the wind. Children were chasing a little crab near the water. Birds flew over the waves.

At the fair, Moana helped carry fruit and baskets. Then she reached into her pocket.

The shell was gone.

"Oh no," Moana said. "I lost it."

She looked on the sand. She looked under the table. She looked near the boats. The shell was not there.

Just then, a small boy came running. He held something in his hand.

"Is this yours?" he asked.

It was the shell. A tiny wave had pushed it into a little pool by the rocks, and the boy had seen it shining in the sun.

Moana smiled with relief. "Yes! Thank you so much."

The boy grinned. "It was hard to find. The sea likes to hide small things."

Moana laughed. She carried the shell back to her grandmother, and her grandmother looked very happy.

"You were brave," she said. "You did not give up."

That afternoon, Moana danced with the children and sang by the sea. The shell stayed safe on the table, and the fair became a beautiful day.

Moana learned that a mistake can feel big, but if you stay calm and ask for help, you can find what is lost.
