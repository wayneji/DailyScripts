# The Late Library Lantern

Harry Potter was in his second year at Hogwarts. One rainy evening, he went to the library after dinner because he wanted a quiet place to read. Hermione was there too, with three big books on the table and a small cup of tea.

"You are late," Hermione said, but she smiled.

Harry sat down. "I only want to finish my history homework," he said.

Outside, the wind moved the tree branches, and the windows made soft sounds. The library felt warm and safe. Then the lights at the far end of the room went out.

Hermione looked up. "That is strange," she said.

A small lantern near the old book shelves was dark. Madam Pince walked over and looked worried.

"This lantern must stay on at night," she said. "It helps the students see the steps near the back door. If it stays dark, someone may fall."

Harry stood up at once. "Can we help?" he asked.

Madam Pince gave him a new candle and some matches. "Try, but be careful," she said.

Harry and Hermione walked to the back of the library. The shelves were tall, and the room was very quiet. Harry tried to light the candle, but the wind from a half-open window blew it out.

"That is the problem," Hermione said. She closed the window, but the old latch was loose. The window opened again.

Then Harry had an idea. He found a thick book and put it against the bottom of the window. Hermione held the candle close, and this time the flame stayed bright.

Harry lit the lantern. Warm yellow light filled the dark corner.

Madam Pince came over and nodded. "Well done," she said. "You saved me a lot of trouble."

Hermione smiled. "It was Harry's idea."

Harry looked at the shining lantern and felt happy. The back steps were safe again, and the library felt calm.

When they returned to their table, Harry said, "Big magic is useful, but small help matters too."

Hermione nodded. "Yes," she said. "And smart thinking can fix many problems."

That night, Harry learned that even a simple light can make a big difference when someone needs it.
