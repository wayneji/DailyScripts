# Arrietty and the Red Button Bridge

## Part 1: A Small Problem Under the Shelf

Arrietty woke up early because the house was very quiet. Quiet was useful for Borrowers. It meant the big people were still asleep, and tiny feet could move across the floor.

She put on her red hair clip and checked her small bag. Inside it, she had a pin, a short brown thread, and two empty acorn cups. Her mother wanted sugar for tea, but Arrietty also wanted to look for a gift for Sho. He had left a clean cloth near the wall last week, and it had helped her family keep warm.

Near the dollhouse steps, Arrietty saw something round and bright. It was a red button, almost as big as a table for her. It had four holes in the middle.

"This can be a bridge base," she whispered.

Spiller climbed down from a chair leg with a grin. "A bridge to where?"

Arrietty pointed to the book shelf. A small pearl bead had rolled into a crack near the sewing box. Homily had lost it that morning. The crack was too wide for a safe jump, but the red button and the brown thread could help them cross.

First, they pulled the button to the edge of the shelf. It was heavy, so they moved slowly. Then Arrietty tied the brown thread through two button holes.

"Hold this end," she said.

Spiller held the thread with both hands. The button did not move. Arrietty smiled. The first part of the bridge was ready.

## Part 2: The Long Walk Across

The bridge was not high, but it looked high to Arrietty. The floor was far below, and a sleepy cat was under the table. Its tail moved once, like a soft rope.

Arrietty put one acorn cup on her head like a helmet. Spiller laughed, but then he put the other cup on his head too.

"Now we are very brave," he said.

They stepped onto the red button. Arrietty pulled the brown thread tight and tied it to a tiny nail in the shelf. Then she made a simple hand line. They could hold it while they crossed the small gap.

Halfway across, Arrietty saw a yellow stamp stuck to the side of a book. It was not useful today, but it looked beautiful.

"After the bead," she said. "No extra borrowing."

Spiller nodded. He liked extra borrowing, but he knew she was right.

Suddenly, the cat opened one eye. Arrietty froze. Spiller froze too. The red button bridge gave a soft click.

Arrietty took the pin from her bag and touched the book gently. Tap, tap, tap. The sound was small, but the cat looked at the other side of the room. Then it stood up and walked away.

"Good idea," Spiller whispered.

They crossed the last part of the bridge and reached the sewing box.

## Part 3: A Gift and a Thank You

The pearl bead was behind a piece of blue cloth. Arrietty rolled it carefully into one acorn cup. It shone like a tiny moon.

On the way back, the button bridge felt easier. They knew each step now. At the dollhouse steps, Homily was waiting with her hands on her hips.

"You went too far," she said.

"But we brought this back," Arrietty answered.

She gave her mother the pearl bead. Homily's face became soft. "That was my best bead."

Arrietty also put two small sugar cubes on the table. She had found them beside the tea tray after the cat left. Homily smiled even more.

Later, Arrietty carried the red button to Sho's cloth. She left it beside the cloth with a tiny note.

Thank you. This was a bridge today.

Sho found the button after breakfast. He did not know all of the story, but he understood the note. He placed the red button on the window sill, where the morning light could shine through its four holes.

Under the floor, Arrietty drank sweet tea from an acorn cup. She felt tired, but happy. A good bridge did not have to be big. It only had to help someone get home.

## Useful A2 Words

- **bridge**: a thing people use to go over water, a road, or a gap.
- **thread**: a long, thin piece of cotton or wool.
- **carefully**: in a way that is slow and safe.
