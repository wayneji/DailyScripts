# Moana: The Lost Lantern

One calm evening, Moana stood near the beach and watched the sea shine under the moon. The waves were soft, and the island was quiet. She had promised to help her grandmother’s old friend, Tala’s sister, prepare a small lantern for a night ceremony.

The lantern was made of wood, shells, and a bright little candle. Moana carried it carefully in both hands. Pua trotted beside her, happy and sleepy at the same time.

“We must not drop it,” Moana said. “This lantern is special.”

Pua gave a small snort, as if he agreed.

They walked along a path near the palm trees. Then a gust of wind came from the sea. The candle went out, and the lantern rolled from Moana’s hands. It went down a short hill and stopped near the rocks.

Moana ran after it, but she saw something else too. A tiny turtle was stuck between two shells near the water. It was trying to reach the sea, but the rocks were too high.

Moana forgot the lantern for a moment. She lifted the shells, made a little path, and guided the turtle toward the water. The turtle moved slowly, then slipped into the waves.

“Now, the lantern,” Moana said.

She found the lantern near the rocks. It was not broken, but the candle was wet. Moana sat on a flat stone and thought. Then she remembered the warm cooking fire near her home. She ran back, took a dry stick, and used it to light the candle again.

Pua watched with wide eyes.

Moana returned to the beach with the glowing lantern. The ceremony began just in time, and everyone smiled when the light shone on the sea.

Her grandmother’s friend said, “You saved both the lantern and the turtle.”

Moana smiled. “The island taught me what to do.”

That night she learned a simple lesson: when you help life around you, the right path often comes back to you.
