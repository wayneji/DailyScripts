# Raya and the River Bridge

Raya walked along a green road with Sisu beside her. The sun was warm, and the hills were bright after the rain. They were going to a small village near the river. Raya had a new plan. She wanted to bring food and water to the people there, because the road to the village had been cut by flood water.

When they arrived, they saw the problem. The old bridge was broken. Big wood pieces floated in the water, and the path across the river was gone. A few villagers stood on one side and looked worried.

Raya crossed her arms. “We need a fast way over,” she said.

Sisu looked at the river and smiled. “Fast is nice, but calm is better. Let’s think.”

Raya did not like waiting, but she listened. Sisu asked the villagers for long ropes and strong bamboo poles. Then Raya found two tall trees near the bank. She tied one rope to each tree, and the villagers helped hold them tight. After that, Sisu used the bamboo poles to make a simple path above the shallow water near the edge. It was not a perfect bridge, but it was safe enough for the people to cross one by one.

The first child walked over and laughed. Then an old woman followed with a basket of fruit. Soon the whole village was on the other side of the river.

Raya felt happy, but she also felt something new. She had wanted to do everything alone. Now she saw that trust could make a hard job easier.

That evening, the village shared rice and soup with Raya and Sisu. The sky turned gold, and the river became quiet.

Raya smiled at Sisu. “I was too fast again.”

Sisu laughed. “And I was too dreamy. But together, we were strong.”

Raya learned that bravery is not only about action. Sometimes bravery is also about asking for help, trusting friends, and building a way forward together.
