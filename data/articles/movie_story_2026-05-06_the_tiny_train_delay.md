# The Tiny Train Delay

Paddington was very excited because he wanted to visit a small town outside London with the Brown family. Mrs. Brown packed sandwiches, Mr. Brown checked the train time three times, and Judy and Jonathan laughed at Paddington's red hat because it looked extra bright that day.

At the station, many people were waiting. Some were reading newspapers, and some were carrying bags. Paddington held his small suitcase and looked around with wide eyes.

"Please stay close," Mrs. Brown said.

"Of course," Paddington answered.

But then Paddington smelled warm buns from a food stand. He walked only a few steps away, but when he came back, he saw a little girl crying near a bench.

Paddington went to her at once. "Excuse me," he said kindly. "Why are you sad?"

The girl wiped her eyes. "My toy train fell under the ticket machine," she said. "My father is buying tickets, and I cannot reach it."

Paddington looked. The toy train was deep under the heavy machine. It was too far for the girl, and even Paddington's arm was not long enough.

Just then, Mr. Brown came over. "What is happening?" he asked.

Paddington pointed at the toy. Mr. Brown knelt down, but he could not reach it either.

Paddington thought for a moment. Then he opened his suitcase and took out a jar of orange marmalade, a spoon, and a long scarf.

Mr. Brown looked confused. "Paddington?"

Paddington smiled. He tied the spoon to the scarf, then carefully moved it under the machine. The spoon touched the toy train and pulled it slowly closer.

"Almost there," Jonathan said.

With one more careful pull, the toy train came out. The little girl clapped her hands. At the same moment, her father returned and thanked them many times.

Then the station speaker announced that their train would be five minutes late.

Mr. Brown smiled. "Well, that worked well for everyone."

Paddington gave the girl a small polite wave and closed his suitcase.

As the family walked to the platform, Mrs. Brown said, "You always find trouble, but also a way to help."

Paddington smiled. "A small delay can be useful," he said.

That day, he learned that kindness can turn waiting time into something wonderful.
