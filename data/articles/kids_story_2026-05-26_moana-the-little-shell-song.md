# Moana and the Little Shell Song

It was a warm day on Motunui. Moana walked near the beach with Pua at her side. The village was getting ready for a small evening song, and Moana had one special job. She needed to bring the bright shell that made the first sound.

The shell was not big, but it was important.

Moana placed it on a rock while she helped carry flowers. Pua sniffed the sand and wagged his tail. Then Moana turned back to the rock.

The shell was gone.

“Oh, no,” she said. “The song starts soon.”

Pua snorted and ran toward the water. Moana followed him past the palm trees and the canoe boats. She looked under a net, behind a basket, and near the water line. But she could not see the shell.

Then she heard a tiny click-clack sound from a crab hole near the shore.

Moana knelt down and looked inside. A small crab was holding the shell with both claws.

“Hello,” Moana said softly. “Did you like the shell?”

The crab opened one eye. It did not run away. It only looked nervous.

Moana smiled. She took a fresh mango leaf from the sand and held it near the hole. The crab came out slowly and touched the leaf. Then it let the shell roll back onto the beach. Moana thanked the crab and left one sweet mango slice near the hole.

Pua gave a happy grunt. Moana picked up the shell and cleaned it with her dress.

When she returned to the village, the song had not started yet. That was good.

Moana blew the shell at the first note, and the sound floated over the water. Everyone smiled.

That night, Moana learned that being kind can help even the shyest little creature do the right thing.
