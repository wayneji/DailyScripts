# The Cold Night at Hogwarts

Harry Potter was in the Gryffindor common room on a cold night. The fire was warm, but the windows were dark, and the wind moved fast outside. Harry had a small job from Professor Lupin. He needed to take a folder of old papers to the library before bedtime.

Hermione wanted to go with him. "It is late," she said. "And the castle feels strange tonight."

Harry smiled. "It is only a short walk."

So they left the common room and walked through the quiet hallways. The candles were low. Long shadows moved on the stone walls. Harry held the folder tight, because the wind came in through an open window and tried to lift the papers.

When they reached the stairway near the third floor, they heard a soft sound. It was not a ghost. It was a little sound, like crying.

Harry and Hermione looked behind a big suit of armor. There they saw a small first-year student from Hufflepuff. She was holding a broom and a broken ribbon.

"I cannot find my way to my room," she said. "And my ribbon flew away. I am scared."

Hermione knelt down and spoke in a kind voice. "Do not worry. We can help you."

Harry looked up and saw the ribbon stuck on a torch holder high on the wall. It was just out of reach. He climbed one step, then another, and used the end of the folder to pull it down.

The girl smiled with relief. "Thank you!"

Hermione showed her the way to her dormitory. Then they all walked together, slowly and safely. The dark hallway felt less cold now.

After that, Harry gave the papers to the librarian. He was late, but Professor Lupin was not angry. He only said, "Helping someone is also a good kind of bravery."

Harry liked those words. On that night at Hogwarts, he learned that a small act of kindness can make a scary place feel warm again.
