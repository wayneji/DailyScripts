# Matilda and the Blue Chalk Star

## Part 1: The Missing Star

Matilda liked the quiet part of the morning best. The classroom was clean, the books were straight, and the sun made warm squares on the floor.

Miss Honey was putting names on the reading board. Each child who finished a book could move a small chalk star higher on the board. Matilda's star was blue. It was small, but it made her feel proud.

Then Miss Honey stopped.

"Oh dear," she said. "The blue star is gone."

Matilda looked at the board. The red stars were there. The white stars were there. But her blue chalk star was missing from the top row.

"Maybe it fell," Matilda said.

The class looked under tables and chairs. Lavender looked inside the pencil pot. Bruce checked near the door. Nobody found it.

Miss Honey smiled, but Matilda could see she was worried. The board was for the school open afternoon. Parents would visit after lunch, and Miss Honey wanted the room to feel happy and ready.

Matilda stood very still. She did not want to make a show. She only wanted to help.

Near the reading corner, she saw a little line of blue dust. It went past the rug and stopped beside a potted fern.

"Miss Honey," Matilda said softly, "may I look near the plant?"

"Of course," said Miss Honey.

Matilda walked to the fern. One green leaf was shaking, but there was no wind in the room.

## Part 2: The Quiet Clue

Matilda knelt beside the pot. The blue dust was on the floor, then on the side of the pot, then on one low leaf. Something had moved the star, but it had not moved far.

Lavender came over. "Is it magic?"

"Maybe it is just a small accident," Matilda said.

She looked behind the pot. There was a narrow space between the shelf and the wall. It was too small for her hand. In that dark space, she saw a tiny blue edge.

"There it is," she whispered.

Miss Honey brought a long ruler, but the ruler was too wide. Bruce brought a paper clip, but it was too short. The class became noisy, and the blue edge slipped deeper into the space.

Matilda took one slow breath. She looked at the star and thought, come forward, please. Just a little.

The blue chalk star moved. Not much. Only a tiny step. Then another.

Matilda quickly put a folded card on the floor. The star slid onto it, soft as a leaf.

"I got it!" she said.

The class cheered. Miss Honey looked at Matilda in her kind, quiet way. She did not ask a big question. She only said, "Thank you. That was very clever."

Matilda smiled. "The dust helped."

## Part 3: Open Afternoon

After lunch, Miss Honey cleaned the blue star with a dry cloth. The star was not perfect anymore. One corner was smaller, and one side had a little scratch.

"It still works," Matilda said.

"Yes," said Miss Honey. "Sometimes a small mark tells a good story."

She placed the blue chalk star back on the top row. Then she asked the class to choose three books for the open afternoon table. Matilda chose a story about a garden. Lavender chose a book of jokes. Bruce chose a book about trains.

When the parents arrived, the classroom looked bright. The reading board was full of stars, and the three books sat neatly on the table.

Matilda's father walked past the board too quickly, but Miss Honey stopped beside it.

"Matilda helped us solve a little mystery today," she said.

Matilda looked down at her shoes. She did not need loud praise. She only liked the warm feeling in her chest.

At the end of the day, Miss Honey gave Matilda a small piece of blue chalk.

"For your own stories," she said.

That night, Matilda drew a blue star on the first page of her notebook. Under it she wrote, A clue can be small, but it can still lead home.

## Useful A2 Words

- **proud**: happy because you did something good.
- **clue**: a small thing that helps you find an answer.
- **scratch**: a small line or mark on something.
